Flyers and posters: scale, legibility and print preparation

How to balance hierarchy and production needs for promotional prints.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~3 MIN READ

A flyer read at arm’s length and a poster viewed across a room need different typographic strategies. The artwork may look attractive on the same laptop screen, but its actual reading distance changes everything. Print preparation should start with size, audience distance and paper stock rather than simply maximizing the output resolution.

Define the reading context

A handed-out A5 flyer can carry details and small copy, while an A2 poster should communicate its main message quickly. Make the event or purpose visible before secondary information.

Use a hierarchy of one headline, limited key facts and a clear action. Do not rely on the audience leaning forward to read essential details.

Design at final aspect ratio

Choose the real intended size early. Resizing a square social post into a tall print format can crop critical elements or force awkward empty space.

If you must repurpose artwork, adjust the composition deliberately. Images and type may need repositioning rather than simply scaling everything up.

Audit photography and graphics

Check each raster image at its placed physical size. A hero photo may be effective at the required viewing distance despite lower PPI, but a tiny logo raster can still look poor.

Preserve vector text and diagrams where possible. Avoid exporting an entire poster as a compressed JPEG if the printer can accept a production PDF.

Respect finishing and distribution

Outdoor display, indoor noticeboards, folded leaflets and hand-to-hand flyers require different paper, coatings and moisture resistance. Select materials with the provider.

Make sure all important text clears the safe margins. If the poster will be framed or mounted, account for visible area lost under the frame.

Review the printed proof from the right distance

Stand at the intended reading distance and judge headline impact, secondary information and color. Check against the source file, especially URLs, dates and location text.

A print-ready technical file is not the same as an effective communication design. Test both legibility and prepress compliance before ordering a full run.

Two formats from one campaign

A campaign requires an A5 handout and an A2 wall poster. The handout has room for an agenda, small map and registration details; the poster is mainly a title, time, place and URL. Simply resizing the flyer to A2 leaves the headline competing with too much fine copy and wastes the poster’s viewing distance advantage.

Create separate compositions from the same visual identity. Keep brand colors, recognizable typography and core image consistent while adjusting hierarchy. Check each raster asset at its final placed size instead of relying on a single export setting across both deliverables.

A practical distance test

Print the poster headline on ordinary paper at approximate real size and stand several steps back. Can you still identify the event and date quickly? Then inspect the handout at arm’s length: are small logistical details comfortable to read, and is the call to action obvious? These are user-experience tests independent of prepress math.

At delivery, confirm final size, trim and bleed with the printer. If the posters will hang in frames, account for the area hidden under a mount. For outdoor banners, add material and mounting considerations, which may be more influential than nominal photographic PPI.

Watch for this mistake

A 300-ppi export will not rescue an unreadably small headline or a poorly organized poster.

Before you send the file

  • Choose size based on viewing distance and display conditions.
  • Recompose for the actual aspect ratio.
  • Check placed-image resolution, bleed and contrast.
  • Proof the headline and key details at intended viewing distance.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
