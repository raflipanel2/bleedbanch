A4, A5 and US Letter: choose paper before layout

A size guide that prevents wrong proportions, unwanted scaling and inconsistent folds.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~4 MIN READ

Many print issues start before artwork exists: the designer selects a canvas that does not match the paper sold or expected by the printer. ISO A-series sheets and North American Letter paper are similar enough to be confused but not interchangeable. Their proportions differ, so automatic “fit to page” printing may scale elements or introduce uneven margins.

Understand the A-series relationship

A4 is 210 × 297 mm and A5 is 148 × 210 mm. The A-series is designed so that halving a sheet along its longer direction approximates the next size with nearly the same aspect ratio. That makes it useful for booklets and folded printed materials.

This does not mean every folded A4 design automatically makes a finished A5 booklet. Folding, trimming, pagination and binding allowance still matter. A multi-page folded piece requires a production imposition plan.

US Letter is a different shape

US Letter is 8.5 × 11 inches, about 215.9 × 279.4 mm. Compared with A4, it is wider and shorter. If you design a form precisely to A4 and print it on Letter with “fit” enabled, the print software may shrink the page and reduce text size.

If a form must be printed internationally, create separate Letter and A4 versions or deliberately design with generous margins. Do not assume the receiving office has both sizes available.

Choose the format from the project

Business letters and reports follow local office standards. Posters and event materials should match available stock or the print shop’s cutting plan. Folded brochures need confirmation of whether the stated size describes a flat sheet or one folded panel.

For a trifold leaflet, panel widths may vary slightly to let the inner flap fold cleanly. Give the printer the intended finished folded size, not only the outside dimensions.

Watch orientation and units

Portrait A4 is 210 × 297 mm, while landscape A4 is 297 × 210 mm. A design may visually rotate without changing the reported print orientation, especially when exporting a PDF. Recheck measurements rather than trusting the appearance of the thumbnail.

Mixed inches and millimeters cause subtle errors. A nominal 3 mm bleed is close to but not precisely identical to 0.125 inches; printers may accept either, but the export should follow the actual production specification.

Confirm the final dimensions in the PDF

Check the PDF page boxes and actual dimensions after export. A PDF titled “A4” can still have a custom-size page. Compare outer PDF dimensions, trim box and requested bleed.

If the job will be cut from a larger sheet, ask about grain direction, paper availability and whether the shop will impose multiple pieces. Changing the document dimensions late can force reflow of every text box.

What a half-sheet fold really means

An A4 sheet measures 210 × 297 mm. Folded once along the longer dimension, it gives an A5-shaped folded product with panels around 148.5 × 210 mm before any finishing trim. If the printer trims the folded item, the actual finished dimensions can be slightly smaller. Do not promise customers a precise finished folded size based solely on the nominal parent sheet.

For a booklet, the pagination has to reflect how sheets nest. Four A5 booklet pages can come from one A4 sheet printed on both sides and folded, but the front/back order is not simply page 1 next to page 2. Let the printer impose or use a verified booklet workflow to avoid inverted pages.

A test for international files

Suppose a colleague prints an A4 checklist on US Letter paper. Since Letter is shorter, the printer may shrink everything to fit. That can make a 10-point form become noticeably smaller and leave odd gaps. If the checklist relies on handwritten response boxes, the change matters beyond appearance.

For a form used in multiple countries, offer two intentionally laid-out files. Keep the main content width and readable type consistent while adjusting margins and page breaks separately. State “Print at 100%” where exact sizing matters, then verify which paper is loaded. Avoid promising an identical appearance with automatic scaling.

Watch for this mistake

An A4 PDF printed on US Letter using “shrink to fit” can appear fine at a glance yet subtly alter typography and margins.

Before you send the file

  • Write the finished size in both units if a printer uses mixed units.
  • Confirm portrait versus landscape and flat versus folded dimensions.
  • Disable automatic “fit to page” for proofs that require exact size.
  • Inspect the PDF’s reported page size after export.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
