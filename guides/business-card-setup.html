Business card preflight: from 90 × 50 mm to final cut

A job-specific checklist for small, high-contrast printed pieces.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~3 MIN READ

Business cards are physically small, so every millimeter matters. The layout has little tolerance for text near the edge, QR codes pushed into corners or overly narrow rules. Start with the intended finished size and make the production geometry explicit before working on the visual style.

Confirm the actual card specification

Common sizes vary by market, such as 90 × 50 mm or 3.5 × 2 inches. Corner rounding, finishes, double-sided printing and paper thickness can change the usable design area.

Ask for final size, bleed, safe area and finish at the beginning. A printer’s die-cut template takes precedence over any size you found on a blog.

Build the bleed correctly

If the card finishes at 90 × 50 mm and the printer requests 3 mm bleed, the outer artwork area becomes 96 × 56 mm. Extend backgrounds and full-bleed graphics through that entire area.

For a card with rounded corners, avoid placing text in the corner region even if it appears to be inside a rectangular safe area.

Design for scanning and readability

Check minimum readable sizes with the intended stock and process. Put a QR code on an uncluttered background with sufficient quiet zone, and test it using several phones after a physical print.

Very thin metallic or reversed lines may not reproduce as a screen suggests. If using foil, spot UV, embossing or die cuts, request separate named layers and a production proof.

Coordinate front and back

For duplex cards, confirm whether back artwork should be rotated or mirrored. Ask whether the printer expects both sides in separate pages or a single imposed sheet.

Keep important elements away from edges on both sides because front-to-back registration can vary. Centering a fine frame equally on both faces is particularly unforgiving.

Approve a real-size proof

Print a trial at 100% size and hold it as a customer would. Check logo clarity, contact information, spacing and whether the chosen surface supports legibility.

If the shop sends a production proof, compare it to the exact final PDF, including the email, phone numbers and any short URLs. Small copy mistakes are not visible from a distant screen preview.

A 90 × 50 mm card with a QR code

Use a hypothetical 90 × 50 mm card with a three-millimeter bleed and four-millimeter safe inset. The background extends to 96 × 56 mm; essential copy stays within 82 × 42 mm. If the client wants a QR code near the bottom-right corner, include its required clear quiet zone within the safe area, not just the visible black-and-white pattern.

Test the QR from a printed sample at the intended size with more than one phone, because screen previews do not reveal every issue with small modules, ink spread and contrast. Confirm that the destination URL works and does not rely on a temporary redirect.

Choose your finish before finalizing layout

Rounded corners can cut into content that looked safe within a rectangular boundary. Foil-stamped logos may require a simplified version of the artwork with thicker strokes. A soft-touch laminate can change perceived contrast, while textured board can make fine lettering less crisp.

A good business-card workflow begins with a material choice, not the last export step. Request the shop’s current template, test one physical proof, and check whether the back will be rotated for the chosen duplex method. Reconfirm the phone number and email immediately before approving production.

Watch for this mistake

A perfectly centered hairline border can look uneven after normal cutting variance; leave more breathing space or remove it.

Before you send the file

  • Confirm the card size, finishing method and corner treatment.
  • Use print-shop bleed and safe-margin values.
  • Test QR readability at physical size.
  • Check duplex orientation and contact details on the exported PDF.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
