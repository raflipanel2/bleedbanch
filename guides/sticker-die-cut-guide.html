Stickers and die cuts: artwork, cut paths and safe zones

Prepare sticker graphics without confusing the cut shape and the printed shape.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~3 MIN READ

A custom-shaped sticker adds another boundary beyond ordinary trim and bleed: the cutting path. That path is a production instruction and should not accidentally print as ink. A successful file makes the separation between artwork, cut contour and safe content clear to the print provider.

Start with the cut path

Ask for a dieline specification: vector path, spot-color name, stroke, offset and layer organization. Some shops provide a template with locked cut paths. Do not redraw the path approximately unless explicitly requested.

For intricate silhouettes, the provider may recommend simplifying tiny notches and internal angles. The material and cutter have physical tolerances.

Plan for bleed around irregular edges

A rectangular image needs to extend beyond the actual sticker cut path, not just the rectangle around it. The required offset depends on the shop. A pattern that stops exactly at the cut line may leave white gaps.

Keep essential text, faces or fine graphics sufficiently far inside the cut. Sharp protrusions and narrow parts can be fragile and may peel poorly.

Understand white ink and clear stock

Transparent stickers may require a white underprint to maintain color visibility. A white-ink plate is often specified as a separate named spot color or layer.

Never assume white areas in a normal CMYK file will receive opaque white ink. Discuss this before design if the artwork relies on a light backing.

Separate printing from finishing

The design file may contain print artwork, cutting contours and finishing notes. Organize these on clearly named layers and follow the provider’s requirements for stroke settings and overprint.

If the cutter requires a hairline path, the exact technical definition is device-specific. Avoid placing duplicate paths that could trigger double cuts.

Request a sample and inspect durability

Choose adhesive type, material and finish for the actual use environment. A paper sticker on a bottle may respond differently to moisture than a vinyl label with laminate.

Inspect a physical sample for corner lift, color shift, legibility and peel behavior. A mockup on a screen cannot predict the material’s actual feel or adhesive performance.

Circular sticker versus shaped sticker

For a simple circular sticker, the printer may provide a circular die template with a requested bleed offset beyond the cut contour. Background artwork should cross that contour everywhere, including narrow outer details. If you place text near the circumference, consider the effective safe zone measured from the curved cut, not the bounding rectangle around the circle.

A highly detailed character-shaped sticker has additional challenges: tiny protrusions and narrow connecting areas may not cut or peel well. Simplifying the silhouette often improves physical durability. Ask for the provider’s minimum cut-feature tolerance, rather than applying arbitrary values from other material types.

A clear vinyl example

A designer uploads a full-color illustration on transparent vinyl and expects white highlights to print as opaque white. Standard process inks may leave those areas transparent unless a white underprint layer is specified. The result can look very different when the sticker is applied to a dark surface.

Discuss substrate, white ink, adhesive, laminate and outdoor exposure in advance. If a white layer is supported, provide it according to the shop’s required naming, separation and trapping instructions. Test a sample on the intended surface before ordering a large batch.

Watch for this mistake

Using a visible colored stroke as a cut path can result in the stroke printing unless it is set up exactly as the vendor requires.

Before you send the file

  • Obtain the shop’s dieline template.
  • Keep cut paths clearly separated from printable ink.
  • Extend artwork beyond the entire cut contour.
  • Check material, adhesive and any white-ink layers.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
