Spot UV, foil and emboss: set up separate finishing artwork

Production layers for special effects need more precision than an on-screen preview.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~3 MIN READ

Special finishes are frequently represented in a mockup as shiny overlays or raised shadows. The production file needs actual vector shapes or masks that specify where the finish will be applied. These shapes may need separate spot-color names, minimum stroke widths and distances from trim.

Treat each finish as a distinct plate or process

Ask the vendor for its required naming convention and geometry. A foil plate, spot UV mask and embossing die are not interchangeable even if the artwork looks similar.

Keep the finishing artwork on separate clearly named layers or supplied templates. Do not merge those paths into the CMYK image or export them as low-resolution raster effects.

Respect registration and minimum details

Special finishes can shift slightly relative to ink. Very fine strokes, tiny reversed holes and precise edge outlines can be difficult to register.

The required minimum line width and gap are provider-specific. Ask for those values rather than copying specifications from unrelated print shops.

Understand what happens at folds and edges

Foil or rigid finishes placed over a crease may crack or behave unpredictably, and thick embossing can change how a piece lays flat.

Keep finish areas away from critical trim or fold features where instructed. Request a physical sample before a large quantity is ordered.

Use mockups to communicate intent, not production

A rendered mockup can show the desired effect but cannot define the die or plate. A press-ready file must include the exact finishing artwork, scale and location.

If the finish must align with a printed logo, check both elements together in a production proof and review the expected alignment tolerance.

Approve a real material sample

Special finishes change with paper, color and viewing light. The same foil over black uncoated board may appear different from foil over a bright coated sheet.

A material proof is especially valuable for branded packaging and premium stationery because a wrong finish can change the entire impression of the product.

Create one mask for each special finish

Imagine a premium business card with a foil logo and spot UV on a pattern behind it. Each finish may require its own clearly named vector mask or separation, and the masks should correspond exactly to the approved artwork position. A JPG preview showing glossy effects is only a communication aid; it cannot instruct a finishing machine where to apply material.

Ask the vendor for sample files illustrating its spot-color names, stroke minimums and knockout rules. Keep those production paths separate from the regular color art so they can be inspected independently before output.

Evaluate tolerance before drawing fine effects

A foil line exactly on the edge of a small printed letter can reveal tiny registration shifts. A densely detailed embossed pattern may not form well on the chosen stock. Instead of forcing an intricate design into the process, enlarge key details, leave practical gaps and obtain an actual material sample.

Finish effects can also change folding behavior and readability. Keep them away from critical creases or fine legal copy when instructed. If the result must match a brand standard, specify the reference sample and review it under realistic lighting.

Watch for this mistake

A screenshot of a metallic logo cannot substitute for a production-ready foil mask.

Before you send the file

  • Get the vendor’s finishing template and naming convention.
  • Use separate vector shapes for each finishing process.
  • Check minimum details and registration tolerance.
  • Approve a sample on the intended stock.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
