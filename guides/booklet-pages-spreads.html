Booklets: pages, spreads, margins and binding allowances

Prepare a multipage PDF without confusing reader spreads and printer imposition.

BY BLEEDBENCH EDITORIAL09 NOV 2026~3 MIN READ

A booklet is not just a stack of landscape pages. Its binding changes the reading order, inner margins and paper behavior. A familiar mistake is exporting two-page reader spreads when the printer needs individual pages and will impose the booklet itself.

Confirm the binding method

Saddle-stitch, perfect binding, coil and case binding each change how pages behave near the spine. Ask the provider for inside margin, bleed and cover requirements before starting.

A saddle-stitch booklet often has a page count in multiples of four. That rule applies to certain folded-signature structures; verify the exact job rather than using it as a universal publishing rule.

Use document pages and facing-page previews

Designing with facing pages helps visualize how content crosses a spread. However, a press-ready export may require single pages in logical reading order so the print provider can handle imposition.

Always distinguish reading spreads from printer spreads. Imposition accounts for the folding and binding process, paper thickness and press sheet size.

Plan inside and outside margins

Text near the gutter can disappear or become hard to read. Inner and outer margins may not be equal, and thick bound books often need more room toward the spine.

Bleed at the spine is handled differently by different workflows. Follow the provider’s document setup rather than extending every page image across the gutter without thinking.

Watch creep and crossovers

In a folded saddle-stitched booklet, inner sheets can extend slightly farther and may be trimmed; this effect is called creep. The provider may adjust imposition to compensate.

Pictures crossing a spread require careful alignment and an understanding of how the pages meet. Ask for a bound dummy when the design relies on precise cross-spread geometry.

Approve a folded or bound proof

Use a physical mockup to check page order, blank pages, cover orientation, spine text and gutters. Check the exported PDF for consistent trim boxes, image quality and font handling.

A multi-page layout has more ways to fail than a single card. A short proof run can reveal problems that are invisible in a scrollable PDF viewer.

Eight-page booklet thought experiment

An eight-page saddle-stitched booklet is often produced from two nested sheets folded and stapled. The pages that print beside each other on a flat sheet do not match consecutive reading order. For example, the outside sheet may pair the back cover with the front cover. That is why manual imposition can produce a confusing result if the designer simply exports facing pages.

Ask whether the printer wants single logical pages or an imposed printer-spread PDF. Modern workflows usually allow the service provider to impose accurately, including creep compensation. A folded paper dummy marked with page numbers can reveal mistakes before any production file is prepared.

Spine and gutter checks

Text that looks safely inside a page margin may become hard to read once pages are bound. Perfect-bound books lose visible space near the glued spine, while saddle-stitched booklets have different creep and inner margin concerns. Fold a rough copy using similar stock thickness if possible.

When artwork crosses two pages, check the physical join as well as the on-screen spread. Misalignment, gutter loss and fold movement can interrupt fine rules or facial features. A single bound sample is more informative than a thousand percent zoom in a PDF viewer.

Watch for this mistake

Do not send manually imposed spreads unless the printer specifically requests them.

Before you send the file

  • Confirm binding type and printer-imposition requirements.
  • Review gutters, inside margins and bleed at the spine.
  • Validate pagination and expected page count.
  • Inspect a folded dummy before a large print run.

Where to verify the details

Production requirements differ among print providers. Check the specific job template and consult the BleedBench reference directory for manufacturer documentation on page boxes, bleed, effective PPI and PDF output. If a client or shop gives a different specification, obtain clarification before export.
